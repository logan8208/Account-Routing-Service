# Territory and routing rules

From the sales manager. These are the rules we use today. Some of this lives in people's heads, so it is written down here for the first time.

## Who is eligible

- Existing customers are off limits. They are owned by the account management team, not by these reps. An account with spend is a customer.
- Companies under 20 employees are too small. Skip them.
- Companies whose core product is a speech recognition engine are competitors. Skip them.
- Companies that no longer exist should obviously not be in anyone's book.
- One company, one account. If the same company shows up more than once, it should end up in one rep's book once, not in three reps' books.

## Segment

- **Enterprise**: 500 or more employees, or the company is public or owned by private equity.
- **Mid-Market**: everyone else who is eligible.

## Region

Region is based on where the company is headquartered. Use the city and state. Do not use the country column on its own, it is wrong often enough that we stopped trusting it.

- **EAST**: Eastern time zone states, plus Ontario, Quebec, and the Canadian maritime provinces.
- **CENTRAL**: Central and Mountain time zone states, plus Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba.
- **WEST**: Pacific time zone states, Alaska and Hawaii, plus British Columbia.

Arizona goes to CENTRAL. Michigan, Indiana, Kentucky and Tennessee go with their state capital's time zone (Michigan and Indiana EAST, Kentucky and Tennessee CENTRAL).

## Assignment

- Each rep covers one region and one segment. See `reps-XLSX.xlsx`.
- Target book size is 260 accounts per rep, including what they already own.
- If a region and segment has more eligible accounts than the rep has room for, the rep gets the highest scoring ones. Tell me how many were left over.
- If a rep has room left after their region is exhausted, do not fill it from another region. Tell me the shortfall.

## What makes an account a good fit

Not a formula, just what we have learned. Weight these however you think is right and tell me why.

- Industry. Healthcare, contact center software, conversation intelligence, telephony, edtech, media, legal, insurance and sales tech are where we win. Everything else is possible but rarer.
- Anything in the notes about calls, recordings, transcription, captions, voice agents, meetings, or IVR is a strong signal.
- Bigger is better, up to a point. Companies over 10,000 employees are slow and usually already have a vendor.
- Recent funding, within the last two years, means budget.
- A rep-created note that says "duplicate?" or "not qualified" should probably count against the account.
